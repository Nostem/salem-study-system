<strong>Initial Conditions:</strong><br>
Operators are performing S1.OP-PT.SW-0101, Differential Pressure Test of Turbine Area SW Stop Valves 11SW20, 13SW20, and 1SW26. The operator in the field identified an incorrect connection point for installation of a pressure instrument.<br>
An OTSC has been initiated to allow the performance test to continue with the proper pressure test connection identified.<br>
The OTSC Initiator has verified the correct revision (Rev. 6), and determined there are no other OTSCs associated with this procedure.<br><br>
<strong>Initiating Cue:</strong><br>
As the operators Supervisor, review the proposed OTSC change. Determine if an OTSC is warranted for the condition. If so, review OTSC for completeness and accuracy. Note any problems, deficiencies, or comments on the OTSC form. If an OTSC is not warranted for this condition, inform the evaluator of this.

<div style="margin-top:12px;padding:10px;background:#052e16;border-left:3px solid #22c55e;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;">
<strong style="color:#22c55e;">Key Decision Point:</strong><br>
<span style="color:#c8cdd5;">Two determinations make this JPM pass/fail. First, the candidate must recognize the <strong>OTSC process is warranted</strong> (the field-identified incorrect connection point means the procedure cannot be performed as written). Second — the discriminating step — the candidate must catch that the <strong>OTSC number is wrong</strong>: per AD-AA-101-101 the OTSC sequential number is the <strong>current procedure revision number followed by the next available sequential letter</strong>. With the procedure at Rev. 6 and no other OTSCs, the correct number is <strong>6A, not the 7A printed on Form F1</strong>. The trap is approving the OTSC for correct intent while overlooking the numbering convention error.</span>
</div>
