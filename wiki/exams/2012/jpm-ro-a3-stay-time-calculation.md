<strong>Initial Conditions:</strong><br>
1. Unit 1 experienced Rx trip with a small RCS leak from 100% power.<br>
2. The Rx failed to automatically trip on a valid trip demand, and was tripped successfully with the Rx trip handle.<br>
3. Salem is currently in an Alert.<br>
4. Your TEDE dose for the year is 1925 mrem.<br><br>
<strong>Initiating Cue:</strong><br>
You have been directed to perform a detailed inspection of 22 RHR pump room prior to starting the pump. You will NOT be going into the 22 RHR HX area. Your job is estimated to take 2 hours to complete. Using the correct survey map, and conservatively using the HIGHEST dose rate in the room for your ENTIRE exposure, determine: 1. Can you complete the job without exceeding your current dose authorization; 2. What would be your new TEDE dose for the year after performing the job (or as much as possible) WITHOUT exceeding your current dose authorization?
</div>

<div style="margin-top:12px;padding:10px;background:#052e16;border-left:3px solid #22c55e;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;">
<strong style="color:#22c55e;">Key Decision Point:</strong><br>
<span style="color:#c8cdd5;">The discriminating recognition is that the <strong>Alert declaration automatically raises the dose authorization to 4500 mrem</strong> (NC.EP-EP.ZZ-0304 Section 5.0 Note) — not the normal administrative limit. Combined with conservatively using the <strong>highest in-room dose rate of 50 mrem/hr</strong> (the square under "Vertical Elbow," excluding the 22 RHR HX area the worker will not enter), the applicant computes 2 hr × 50 = 100 mrem and a new TEDE of <strong>2025 mrem</strong>, well below 4500. The trap is applying a normal exposure limit or picking a dose rate from the wrong room/area. Concluding the job cannot be done, or producing a TEDE other than 2025 mrem, fails the task.</span>
