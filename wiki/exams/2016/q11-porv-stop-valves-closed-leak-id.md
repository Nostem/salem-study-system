<span style="font-size:10px;padding:2px 8px;background:#0d1220;color:#6b7280;border-radius:3px;">000027 K3.03 (3.7)</span>

<div style="font-size:13px;color:#9ca3af;margin-bottom:14px;line-height:1.7;">
<strong>Given:</strong> When responding to lowering PZR pressure IAW S2.OP-AB.PZR-0001, Pressurizer Pressure Malfunction, why are BOTH PZR PORV Stop valves closed when attempting to determine if the PORVs are the source of the pressure reduction?
</div>

<div style="margin-bottom:14px;">
<div style="padding:10px 14px;border-left:3px solid #1a2035;margin-bottom:4px;font-size:12px;color:#c8cdd5;background:#0d1220;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;"><strong>A.</strong> Because the PORV tailpipe temperature sensor is on the combined PORV discharge line.</div>
<div style="padding:10px 14px;border-left:3px solid #1a2035;margin-bottom:4px;font-size:12px;color:#c8cdd5;background:#0d1220;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;"><strong>B.</strong> To prevent pressurizing the PRT past the point where venting will be unavailable due to the high pressure interlock.</div>
<div style="padding:10px 14px;border-left:3px solid #1a2035;margin-bottom:4px;font-size:12px;color:#c8cdd5;background:#0d1220;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;"><strong>C.</strong> Because the PZR PORV and PZR Safety Valves discharge into a single line, and isolation of both PORVs is necessary to determine whether the leak is from the PORV's or Safeties.</div>
<div style="padding:10px 14px;border-left:3px solid #1a2035;margin-bottom:4px;font-size:12px;color:#c8cdd5;background:#0d1220;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;"><strong>D.</strong> Because the PZR PORV and Rx Head Vent Valves discharge into a single line, and isolation of both PORVs will give indication of whether the leak is from the PORV's or Rx Head Vents.</div>
</div>

<div style="padding:8px 12px;background:#052e16;border-left:3px solid #22c55e;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;margin-bottom:10px;color:#c8cdd5;"><strong style="color:#22c55e;">✓ A. Correct.</strong> 55.41.b(7) As shown on dwg 203301, both the PZR PORVs and Safeties discharges combine into a single line going to the PRT. The Safeties each have their own individual temperature sensor, while the PORVs have one sensor on a common line. Since there is no way to distinguish which valve is leaking, both PORV Stop valves are closed. If temperature does not lower, the user is directed to reopen one PORV Stop. If temperature does not rise, then the procedure is exited. If it does, then that stop valve is closed and the other opened. Then the procedure is exited."</div>
<div style="padding:8px 12px;border-left:3px solid #f87171;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;margin-bottom:6px;color:#9ca3af;"><strong style="color:#f87171;">✗ B.</strong> B is incorrect but plausible because discharge is directed to the PRT, and PRT vent valve is prevented from opening >10 psig.</div>
<div style="padding:8px 12px;border-left:3px solid #f87171;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;margin-bottom:6px;color:#9ca3af;"><strong style="color:#f87171;">✗ C.</strong> C is incorrect but plausible because the all the discharges do combine into a single line, however all 3 safeties have their own sensors.</div>
<div style="padding:8px 12px;border-left:3px solid #f87171;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;margin-bottom:6px;color:#9ca3af;"><strong style="color:#f87171;">✗ D.</strong> C is incorrect but plausible because the all the discharges do combine into a single line, however, Rx Head Vent leakage is not addressed in AB.PZR-1, as the vents would be seen as a RCS leak. <em>(Note: source PDF labels this distractor block "C" — apparent typo; content addresses choice D as written.)</em></div>
