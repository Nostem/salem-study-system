<strong>Initial Conditions:</strong><br>
1. You have a maximum of 5 minutes to review the Emergency Operating Procedures used during this scenario to refresh your memory of all events/paths. At the end of your review you will become the Shift Manager (SM). Inform the Evaluator when you are ready to assume SM duties. You may continue to reference the procedures or to look at the control board but "the clock will be running." If there are multiple ECG calls, classify the most severe.<br><br>
<strong>Initiating Cue:</strong><br>
You are the Duty SM. Classify the event, complete the correct ECG Attachment and provide an ICMF to the Primary Communicator. This is a Time Critical JPM.

<div style="margin-top:12px;padding:10px;background:#052e16;border-left:3px solid #22c55e;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;">
<strong style="color:#22c55e;">Key Decision Point:</strong><br>
<span style="color:#c8cdd5;">The discriminating element is recognizing that <strong>TWO</strong> fission product barriers are in a Potential Loss condition for the ESG-3 scenario: <strong>FB2.P (Potential Loss of the Fuel Clad Barrier) AND RB2.P (Potential Loss of the RCS Barrier)</strong>. A single-barrier Potential Loss is only an Alert; the escalation to a <strong>Site Area Emergency (SAE)</strong> comes from <strong>both</strong> the fuel-clad and the RCS barriers being potentially lost simultaneously (using ECG Attachment 3, EP-SA-111-F3). Description of Event = "Potential Loss of the Fuel Clad Barrier AND Potential Loss of the Reactor Coolant System Barrier." ICMF Section III is checked <strong>NO</strong> (no radiological release in progress for ESG-3). Section IV wind speed/direction is retrieved from SPDS. The two regulatory time-critical clocks are: (1) <strong>15 minutes from event recognition to SAE declaration</strong>, and (2) <strong>15 minutes from declaration to ICMF delivery to the Primary Communicator</strong>. Classifying this as a single-barrier Alert (or missing either 15-minute window) fails the task.</span>
</div>
