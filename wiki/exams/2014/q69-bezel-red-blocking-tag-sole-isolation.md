<span style="color:#60a5fa;font-size:14px;font-weight:600;">Q69 — Bezel Red Blocking Tag as Sole Isolation Point</span>
<span style="font-size:10px;padding:2px 8px;background:#0d1220;color:#6b7280;border-radius:3px;">2.2.13 (RO 4.1 / SRO 4.3)</span>
</div>

<div style="font-size:13px;color:#9ca3af;margin-bottom:14px;line-height:1.7;">
<strong>Given:</strong><br>
Of the following, which is the ONLY choice which identifies when a Bezel Red Blocking Tag (RBT) may be used as the sole isolation point?
</div>

<div style="margin-bottom:14px;">
<div style="padding:10px 14px;border-left:3px solid #1a2035;margin-bottom:4px;font-size:12px;color:#c8cdd5;background:#0d1220;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;"><strong>A.</strong> When the tagged position of the component is in the fail-safe position.</div>
<div style="padding:10px 14px;border-left:3px solid #1a2035;margin-bottom:4px;font-size:12px;color:#c8cdd5;background:#0d1220;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;"><strong>B.</strong> When the component being tagged is inside the boundary of another tagging request.</div>
<div style="padding:10px 14px;border-left:3px solid #1a2035;margin-bottom:4px;font-size:12px;color:#c8cdd5;background:#0d1220;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;"><strong>C.</strong> When the location of the component being tagged ensures it won't be operated locally.</div>
<div style="padding:10px 14px;border-left:3px solid #1a2035;margin-bottom:4px;font-size:12px;color:#c8cdd5;background:#0d1220;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;"><strong>D.</strong> When no other means to isolate are practical to establish a Test Boundary, as long as a hazard to personnel or equipment does not exist due to energized sources.</div>
</div>

<details>
<summary style="cursor:pointer;color:#60a5fa;font-size:12px;font-weight:600;padding:8px 0;">▶ Show Answer &amp; Explanation</summary>
<div style="margin-top:10px;padding:16px;background:#0a0e17;border:1px solid #1a2035;border-radius:6px;font-size:12px;">
<div style="padding:8px 12px;background:#052e16;border-left:3px solid #22c55e;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;margin-bottom:10px;color:#c8cdd5;"><strong style="color:#22c55e;">✓ D. Correct.</strong> The specific conditions under which a bezel RBT may be used as the sole isolation point are listed in procedure OP-AA-109-115, Safety Tagging Operations (page 72). A bezel RBT may be used as the sole isolation point when no other means to isolate are practical to establish a Test Boundary, as long as a hazard to personnel or equipment does not exist due to energized sources.</div>
<div style="padding:8px 12px;border-left:3px solid #f87171;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;margin-bottom:6px;color:#9ca3af;"><strong style="color:#f87171;">✗ A.</strong> A, B, and C are not allowed. The tagged position being in the fail-safe position is not one of the listed conditions permitting a bezel RBT as the sole isolation point.</div>
<div style="padding:8px 12px;border-left:3px solid #f87171;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;margin-bottom:6px;color:#9ca3af;"><strong style="color:#f87171;">✗ B.</strong> A, B, and C are not allowed. A component being inside the boundary of another tagging request is not one of the listed conditions permitting a bezel RBT as the sole isolation point.</div>
<div style="padding:8px 12px;border-left:3px solid #f87171;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;margin-bottom:6px;color:#9ca3af;"><strong style="color:#f87171;">✗ C.</strong> C is incorrect because, for example, a component which would be considered inaccessible, i.e., an overhead environment, while normally inaccessible, could still be accessed.</div>
