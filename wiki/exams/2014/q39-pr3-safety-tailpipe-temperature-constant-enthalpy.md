<span style="color:#60a5fa;font-size:14px;font-weight:600;">Q39 — PR3 Safety Stuck Open — Expected Tail Pipe Temperature Indication</span>
<span style="font-size:10px;padding:2px 8px;background:#0d1220;color:#6b7280;border-radius:3px;">010000 K5.02 (RO 2.6 / SRO 3.0)</span>

<div style="font-size:13px;color:#9ca3af;margin-bottom:14px;line-height:1.7;">
<strong>Given:</strong><br>
Given the following conditions:<br><br>
- PZR safety valve PR3 is stuck slightly open.<br>
- Charging pumps are maintaining RCS pressure at 1910 psig.<br>
- PZR vapor space temperature is 630°F.<br>
- The PRT level and pressure are 75% and 5 psig respectively.<br>
- A NCO notes that the tail pipe temperature for PR3 indicates 310°F. He states that he believes that there is a problem with the indication since it is not reading as he expects for the current conditions.<br><br>
What should the indication read?
</div>

<div style="margin-bottom:14px;">
<div style="padding:10px 14px;border-left:3px solid #1a2035;margin-bottom:4px;font-size:12px;color:#c8cdd5;background:#0d1220;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;"><strong>A.</strong> 162°F.</div>
<div style="padding:10px 14px;border-left:3px solid #1a2035;margin-bottom:4px;font-size:12px;color:#c8cdd5;background:#0d1220;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;"><strong>B.</strong> 228°F.</div>
<div style="padding:10px 14px;border-left:3px solid #1a2035;margin-bottom:4px;font-size:12px;color:#c8cdd5;background:#0d1220;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;"><strong>C.</strong> 310°F.</div>
<div style="padding:10px 14px;border-left:3px solid #1a2035;margin-bottom:4px;font-size:12px;color:#c8cdd5;background:#0d1220;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;"><strong>D.</strong> 630°F.</div>
</div>

<div style="margin-top:10px;padding:16px;background:#0a0e17;border:1px solid #1a2035;border-radius:6px;font-size:12px;">
<div style="padding:8px 12px;background:#052e16;border-left:3px solid #22c55e;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;margin-bottom:10px;color:#c8cdd5;"><strong style="color:#22c55e;">✓ B. Correct.</strong> With the PZR at 1910 and 630, the liquid is saturated (page 13 of steam tables). Since throttling is a constant enthalpy process, the downstream must be saturated for the pressure in the PRT. With 5 psig (20 psia), steam tables show a sat temp of 227.918 °F for 20 psia. (page 11).</div>
<div style="padding:8px 12px;border-left:3px solid #f87171;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;margin-bottom:6px;color:#9ca3af;"><strong style="color:#f87171;">✗ A.</strong> The 162 distracter is if psia is used for PRT press vs psig (i.e., taking the PRT 5 psig as 5 psia rather than 20 psia).</div>
<div style="padding:8px 12px;border-left:3px solid #f87171;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;margin-bottom:6px;color:#9ca3af;"><strong style="color:#f87171;">✗ C.</strong> 310 is value given in stem (the indication the NCO is questioning), not the correct expected saturation temperature for the PRT pressure.</div>
<div style="padding:8px 12px;border-left:3px solid #f87171;border-radius:0 4px 4px 0;margin-bottom:6px;color:#9ca3af;"><strong style="color:#f87171;">✗ D.</strong> 630 is what the PZR vapor space temperature is, not the tailpipe (downstream) saturation temperature at PRT pressure.</div>
