# Waste Liquid

## Function & Design Basis

The Liquid Waste System (LWS) provides controlled handling and disposal of liquid wastes generated during plant operation; it is designed to minimize exposure to plant personnel and the general public, in accord with NRC regulations (UFSAR §11.2). The design objectives are to maintain annual activity releases within the limits specified in 10CFR20, to maintain radioactive releases as low as practicable, to collect radioactive and potentially radioactive liquid wastes, to provide processing such that operation and availability of the stations are not limited, and to assure that public exposures are maintained below the design objectives of Appendix I to 10CFR50 (UFSAR §11.2.1).

Radioactive fluids entering the LWS are collected in tanks until subsequent treatment can be determined; they are sampled and analyzed to determine the quantity of radioactivity, processed as required, and then released under controlled conditions following isotopic analysis (UFSAR §11.2.1). Discharge streams are appropriately monitored and safety features are incorporated to preclude releases in excess of the limits of 10CFR20 (UFSAR §11.2.1). The bulk of the radioactive liquid discharged from the Reactor Coolant System (RCS) is processed and retained inside the plant by the CVCS recycle train, which minimizes liquid input to the LWS so that the LWS processes relatively small quantities of generally low-activity wastes (UFSAR §11.2.2). The processed water, from which most of the radioactive material has been removed, is discharged through a monitored line to the service water discharge header and then to the circulating water discharge (UFSAR §11.2.2).

The system is capable of processing all liquid wastes generated during continuous operation of the primary system assuming fission products escape to the reactor coolant by diffusion through defects in the cladding on one percent of the fuel rods (UFSAR §11.2.2). Normal liquid process capacity is <span class="hi">16.5 gpm</span> (Unit 1 evaporator, now inactive); the portable processing system normal process capacity is <span class="hi">25-40 gpm</span> (UFSAR Table 11.2-1).

## Key Components

- **Reactor Coolant Drain Tank (RCDT):** One per unit, located inside containment; a right circular cylinder of welded stainless steel with spherically dished heads, <span class="hi">565 gal</span>, design pressure <span class="hi">25 psig</span>, design temperature <span class="hi">267°F</span> (UFSAR §11.2.3, Table 11.2-4). It receives recyclable waste from reactor coolant pump seal and head tank leakoffs, drains from each of the four primary coolant loops, reactor vessel flange leakage, accumulator drains, excess letdown, and refueling canal drains (UFSAR §11.2.3). During normal operation a nitrogen blanket at <span class="hi">0.5 psig</span> is maintained in the RCDT, which is normally vented to the Gaseous Waste Disposal System vent header so that changes in liquid level cause the tank to breathe to and from this header, eliminating release of hydrogen and radioactive gases to containment (UFSAR §11.2.3). RCDT contents can be transferred to the CVCS holdup tanks, the ECCS RWST, or the LWS holdup tanks; normally all waste is transferred to the CVCS holdup tanks by the Nos. 11, 21 and 12, 22 reactor coolant drain pumps, whose operation is automatically controlled by tank level instrumentation (UFSAR §11.2.3).
- **Reactor coolant drain tank pumps:** Two per unit (located inside containment), horizontal centrifugal canned, rated <span class="val-normal">50 gpm</span> at <span class="hi">175 ft</span> head, design pressure <span class="hi">150 psig</span>, design temperature <span class="hi">200°F</span>, stainless steel; operated automatically by an RCDT level controller, also returning water from the refueling canal and cavity to the refueling water storage tank via a 2-inch line (UFSAR §11.2.2, §11.2.3, Table 11.2-4).
- **Waste Holdup Tanks (WHUTs):** Two per unit, horizontal welded stainless steel, <span class="hi">25000 gal</span> each, design pressure <span class="hi">15 psig</span>, design temperature <span class="hi">180°F</span> (UFSAR §11.2.3, Table 11.2-4). They accept liquid wastes from the CVCS, sump tank, chemical drain tank, reactor coolant drain tank, Steam Generator Blowdown System, floor drains, and hot shower tanks; individual air-operated valves in the common inlet manifold divert flow from one tank to the other so that one tank accepts waste while the other is held to await processing, providing holdup time to allow shorter-lived radionuclides to decay (UFSAR §11.2.3). An additional <span class="hi">25000-gal</span> waste monitor holdup tank is available to accept waste surges in the event of an emergency (UFSAR §11.2.3).
- **Waste Monitor Holdup Tank (3rd WHUT):** One per unit, horizontal welded stainless steel, <span class="hi">25000 gal</span>, design pressure <span class="hi">15 psig</span>, design temperature <span class="hi">180°F</span>; serves a dual purpose — normal function as a third waste holdup tank to receive abnormally large quantities of waste, and it can also serve as a waste monitor tank (UFSAR §11.2.3, Table 11.2-4).
- **Waste Monitor Tanks:** Two per unit, vertical stainless steel, <span class="hi">950 gal</span> each — these tanks (and their pumps) have been disabled and abandoned in place at the West end of the 84 ft elevation (UFSAR §11.2.3, Table 11.2-4).
- **Containment Sump Tank and pumps:** One sump tank per unit, horizontal welded stainless steel, <span class="hi">1000 gal</span>, design pressure <span class="hi">15 psig</span> (UFSAR Table 11.2-4). The containment sump accumulates all floor drains, washdowns from refueling decontamination operations, drains and condensate from the fan coil units, and miscellaneous potentially radioactive but non-reactor-coolant equipment drains; two sump pumps pump the contents directly to the waste holdup tanks under sump-level control, and both pumps can also be started manually (UFSAR §11.2.3). (The Auxiliary Building Sump Tank receives floor drains from locations above it on the 64 ft elevation — see Exam — 2012 Q60 below.)
- **Laundry and Hot Shower Tanks:** Two stainless steel tanks, <span class="hi">600 gal</span> each, common to both units' LWS, collecting liquid wastes from the hot shower and local sinks; one tank fills while the other is pumped to a waste holdup tank, with a basket-type strainer downstream to prevent discharge of lint; pump started/stopped manually from a local control panel (UFSAR §11.2.3, Table 11.2-4).
- **Chemical Drain Tank:** One per unit, vertical stainless steel, <span class="hi">600 gal</span>, collecting drainage from the chemistry laboratory; this potentially high-activity waste is normally transferred to the waste holdup tanks to await processing, pump started/stopped from a local control panel; the chemical drain and laundry tank suction lines are interconnected so the laundry and chemical drain pumps can substitute for each other (UFSAR §11.2.3, Table 11.2-4).
- **Spent Resin Storage Tank:** One per unit, vertical stainless steel, <span class="hi">300 ft³</span>, design pressure <span class="hi">100 psig</span>; retains spent resin discharged from the mixed bed, evaporator feed ion exchange, spent fuel pit, deborating vessel, and cation demineralizers, with a water layer maintained to prevent resin degradation; contents removed by flushing with nitrogen (UFSAR §11.2.3, Table 11.2-4).
- **Waste Evaporator:** The Unit 1 waste evaporator is abandoned in place; installation of the Unit 2 waste evaporator has been cancelled (UFSAR §11.2.3).
- **Portable Processing System:** A portable system installed on the 100 ft elevation of the truck bay of the Auxiliary Building can process liquid radwaste from either unit; its effluent returns to either the Waste Monitor Holdup Tanks or the CVCS Monitor Tanks to be sampled, analyzed, and either reprocessed or disposed of; exhausted ion-exchange media and/or filtration is transferred to an approved burial container (UFSAR §11.2.3). Portable Demineralizer normal process capacity is <span class="hi">28 gpm</span> (Unit 1) and <span class="hi">38 gpm</span> (Unit 2) (UFSAR Table 11.2-1).
- **Piping and Valves:** Piping and valves in contact with liquid wastes are stainless steel, welded except where flanged connections are required for maintenance; relief valves are provided for tanks containing radioactive wastes that might be overpressurized by improper operation or component malfunction (UFSAR §11.2.3). All LWS equipment is located in the Auxiliary Building except the reactor coolant drain tank and drain tank pumps (in containment) and the 2-inch line from the drain tank pumps to the RWST (UFSAR §11.2.2).

## Automatic Features & Setpoints

Although the Waste Monitor Hold-up or CVCS Monitor Tank analysis forms the basis for recording activity releases, the radiation monitor provides surveillance over the operation by closing the discharge valve if the liquid activity exceeds a preset value (UFSAR §11.2.2). The monitored waste released from the LWS is normally pumped into the Circulating Water System discharge lines via the Service Water System; the maximum release rates are calculated based on how many circulators are available in the release path, and the release rate is controlled by throttling the discharge valve to maintain the maximum release rate or less (UFSAR §11.2.8). Liquid waste releases are continuously monitored for gross activity during discharges to ensure the activity limits specified in 10CFR20 for unrestricted areas are not exceeded (UFSAR §11.2.4).

## Design Features & Interlocks

At least two valves must be manually opened to permit discharge of liquid waste from the LWS, and the control valve will trip closed on a high effluent radioactivity level signal (UFSAR §11.2.2). Verification is made to ensure that dilution flow sufficient to meet the requirements of 10CFR20 is available whenever radioactive liquid wastes are released to the Plant Discharge System; the maximum allowable release at the plant is specified in the Technical Specifications (UFSAR §11.2.4).

Following containment isolation, valves WL-12 and WL-13 in the reactor coolant drain tank pump discharge line are maintained in the shut position until manually reset by the operator (UFSAR §11.2.3). The RCDT nitrogen blanket and its automatic vent to the Gaseous Waste Disposal System vent header (and transfer of RCDT contents under tank-level control) provide automatic realignment of recyclable waste to the CVCS holdup tanks during normal operation (UFSAR §11.2.3).

## Interconnections & Loads

During normal plant operation the LWS processes liquids from equipment drains and leaks, radioactive chemical laboratory drains, hot shower drains, decontamination area drains, CVCS demineralizer regenerant solutions and spent resins, and the Sampling System (UFSAR §11.2.2). In addition, piping directs potential fluid leakage from valves in the Residual Heat Removal (RHR), Safety Injection (SIS), Containment Spray, CVCS, and Sampling Systems to the LWS to minimize spread of highly radioactive liquid following postulated accidents (UFSAR §11.2.2). The LWS also collects and transfers liquids — depending on fluid content directly to the CVCS, to the waste holdup tanks, or back to the refueling water storage tank — from the reactor coolant loops, the pressurizer relief tank, reactor coolant pump secondary seals, excess letdown (during startup), accumulators, and valve and reactor vessel flange leakoffs (UFSAR §11.2.2).

The estimated annual liquid discharge to the LWS includes reactor-grade sources (tank drains, filter strainer drains, heat exchanger drains, demineralizers, valve leakoffs, pump leakages, sample sink drains) and nonreactor-grade sources (Component Cooling Heat Exchanger drains, Component Cooling valve leakoffs, hot showers, decontamination and floor scrubbing, and BAE distillate) (UFSAR Table 11.2-5). On discharge, monitored waste is normally pumped into the Circulating Water System discharge lines via the Service Water System (UFSAR §11.2.8). Continuous releases from the steam generator blowdown system are sampled, monitored, and controlled in accordance with the Salem Offsite Dose Calculation Manual; the steam generator radiation monitors provide a signal to close the isolation valves to maintain releases within the ODCM (UFSAR §11.2.4, §11.2.6).

## Control-Room Operation

The system is controlled from a central panel in the Auxiliary Building; malfunction of the system is alarmed in the Auxiliary Building and annunciated in the Control Room (UFSAR §11.2.2). Radioactive liquid batch wastes are sampled prior to releases to the Plant Discharge System and records of all releases are kept (UFSAR §11.2.4). Samples are taken on each batch of liquid waste released; station records contain the quantity and concentration of radioactive isotopes, the volume of each batch, and estimates of the water flow for dilution (UFSAR §11.2.5).

## Solid Radwaste System

Processes and packages solid radioactive waste including spent resins, filter cartridges, dry active waste, and evaporator concentrates for storage and offsite disposal. (UFSAR 11.5)

## Offsite Radiological Monitoring

Environmental monitoring program samples air, water, soil, vegetation, fish, and shellfish around the plant to verify that radiological releases are within regulatory limits. (UFSAR 11.6)
