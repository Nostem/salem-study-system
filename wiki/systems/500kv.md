# 500KV

## Function & Design Basis

Provides offsite AC power to all station auxiliaries. Each unit generates electric power at <span class="val-normal">25 kV</span> which is fed through an isolated phase bus to the main transformer bank where it is stepped up to <span class="val-normal">500 kV</span> and delivered to the switching station (UFSAR §8.2.1). The 500-kV switching station design incorporates a breaker-and-a-half scheme for high reliability and is connected to three 500-kV transmission lines (UFSAR §8.2.1).

The station has been designed to be capable of being safely shut down from full power in the event of the loss of all offsite power sources; redundant and independent onsite power sources are provided to ensure availability of the necessary power for shutdown systems. Total loss of all onsite and offsite AC power is not a design basis event (UFSAR §8.1.4.1). The distribution system and the network interconnections are designed with sufficient independence, redundancy, capacity, and testability to provide reliable power to unit auxiliaries during startup, operation, and shutdown (UFSAR §8.1.4.1).

Reliability is provided by: each of the three transmission lines taking a separate route to its destination; the breaker-and-a-half switching scheme; primary and backup relaying with circuit breaker failure protection for each circuit; and two independent DC circuits for each 500-kV breaker from two independent, separate sources of DC control power supplied to the 500-kV switchyard from the station batteries — loss of either DC source will not prevent connection of the station auxiliary power system to a 500-kV source (UFSAR §8.2.2).

## Key Components

- **Main generator (one per unit):** <span class="val-normal">25 kV</span>, 1300 MVA, 0.9 PF (UFSAR Figure 8.3-1).
- **No. 1 generator main transformers (Unit 1):** 288.7/24 kV, 416.7/466.7 MVA; **No. 2 generator main transformers (Unit 2):** 288.7/24 kV, 352.5/440 MVA — step the generator output up to 500 kV (UFSAR Figure 8.3-1).
- **Station power transformers T1–T4:** 500-13.8 kV, 60/80/100 MVA (112 MVA at 65°C); connected to different bus sections of the 500-kV switching station, each with the capacity to start both units at the same time (UFSAR §8.3.1.1, Figure 8.3-2a). These step 500 kV down to the 13-kV ring/bus sections that feed the plant distribution buses.
- **Auxiliary power transformer (25-4-4 kV):** primary side connected to the generator isolated phase bus; provides the alternate (generator-derived) source to the 4160-V group buses during normal operation (UFSAR §8.3.1.1, §8.3.1.2).
- **500-kV switching station:** breaker-and-a-half scheme, connected to three 500-kV transmission lines (UFSAR §8.2.1).

**Transmission lines and switching stations (UFSAR §8.2.1):**

- Two transmission lines go north via separate rights-of-way to two major switching stations: PSE&G **New Freedom** Switching Station and Atlantic City Electric's **Orchard** Switching Station.
- New Freedom is solidly connected to the PSE&G 230-kV bulk power system via four 500/230-kV autotransformers. Orchard is connected to Atlantic City Electric's 230-kV bulk power system via an autotransformer and to the Pennsylvania/New Jersey/Maryland 500-kV interconnected system.
- The third transmission line serves as a tie line to the adjacent **Hope Creek** 500-kV switchyard, which is also integrated into the Pennsylvania/New Jersey/Maryland 500-kV interconnected system. All three 500-kV power lines are available for either or both units.

## Operation

During startup the group buses are energized through the station power transformers; after the generator is synchronized to the 500-kV system, the group buses are manually transferred to the 25-4-4 kV auxiliary power transformer. Should a unit trip, each 4160-V group bus automatically transfers from the auxiliary power transformer source back to the station power transformer (offsite) source (UFSAR §8.3.1.2). There are no present plans to incorporate automatic load dispatching for the Salem units (UFSAR §8.2.1).

System network performance has been analyzed on a computer model for critical three-phase faults cleared by primary relay protection; the Salem units are stable for loss of one Salem unit, loss of the largest generating unit on the grid, and loss of the most critical transmission line. A fault on the Salem-Hope Creek 500-kV line is the most critical line fault in evaluating Salem stability, and for a fault on this line the Salem units remain stable (UFSAR §8.2.2). For certain unexpected operating conditions (e.g., the East Windsor–New Freedom or Hope Creek–Red Lion 500-kV line out of service), system stability studies indicate that tripping one of the Salem units is required to maintain system stability; the relay protection circuitry was modified to incorporate this (UFSAR §8.2.2).

These considerations minimize the possibility of loss of more than one offsite power source. In the event of loss of all offsite power sources, the Engineered Safeguards Systems are supplied from the standby AC power supply (the emergency diesel generators) (UFSAR §8.2.2).
