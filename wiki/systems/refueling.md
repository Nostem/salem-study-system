# Refueling

## Function & Design Basis

The Fuel Handling System consists of the equipment and structures used to handle new and spent fuel assemblies in a safe manner during refueling and fuel transfer operations (UFSAR §9.1.4). It is shown on Figure 9.1-5 (UFSAR §9.1.4). The design conforms to the recommendations of Regulatory Guide 1.13 (UFSAR §9.1.4).

The reactor is refueled with equipment designed to handle spent fuel under water from the time it leaves the reactor until it is placed in a cask for storage at the Independent Spent Fuel Storage Installation (ISFSI) or shipment from the site (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.1). Underwater transfer of spent fuel provides an effective, economic and transparent radiation shield as well as a reliable cooling medium for removal of decay heat; boric acid is added to the water to ensure subcritical conditions during refueling (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.1).

Two significant points are assured by the refueling procedure (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.2):
- The refueling water and the reactor coolant contain sufficient boron concentration such that, together with the control rods, the core is kept approximately <span class="hi-exam">5 percent Δk/k subcritical</span> during refueling (required refueling boron concentration is reported in the COLR) (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.2).
- The water level in the refueling canal is high enough to keep radiation levels within acceptable limits when fuel assemblies are being removed from the core, and also provides adequate cooling for the fuel during transfer operations (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.2).

Refueling of one unit places no restrictions on operation of the other unit and does not affect the safety aspects of the other unit (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.2).

**Seismic classification:** Fuel Handling System components are generally NOT designed to Class I (seismic) requirements because they do not fit the definition of Class I structures; those components are designed to Class III requirements (UFSAR §9.1.4). The spent fuel racks, spent fuel pool, and spent fuel pool bridge structure, however, ARE designed to Class I (seismic) requirements; other Fuel Handling System components are not required to operate following a design basis seismic event (UFSAR §9.1.4).

## Key Components

### Major Structures Required for Refueling

- **Reactor Cavity** — a reinforced concrete structure that forms a pool above the reactor when filled with borated water for refueling. It is filled to a depth that limits radiation at the surface of the water to <span class="val-normal">2.5 mR/hr</span> during the brief periods when a fuel assembly is transferred over the reactor vessel flange (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.4). The reactor vessel flange is sealed to the bottom of the cavity by a cavity seal installed prior to flooding. The cavity is large enough to store the reactor upper and lower internals, the control cluster drive shafts, and miscellaneous refueling tools; its floor and sides are lined with stainless steel (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.4).
- **Refueling Canal** — a passageway extending from the reactor cavity to the inside surface of the reactor containment, formed by two concrete shielding walls. The canal floor is at a lower elevation than the reactor cavity to provide the greater depth required for the fuel transfer upending device and the control cluster changing fixture. The transfer tube enters the containment and protrudes through the end of the canal; canal wall and floor linings are similar to the reactor cavity (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.4).
- **Decontamination Facilities** — a decontamination pit in the fuel handling area for decontaminating spent fuel shipping casks prior to truck loading. It is also used to prepare the multi-purpose canister (MPC) for dry storage at the ISFSI: the HI-TRAC transfer cask is moved from the fuel transfer pool to the pit, where the MPC lid is welded to the canister shell and the MPC fuel cavity is drained, dried, and backfilled with helium (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.4).
- **New Fuel Storage Pit** — a dry pit with safe-geometry storage racks in the fuel handling area for storage of approximately one-third of a core, located outside the area over which a spent fuel transfer/shipping cask may travel (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.4).
- **Fuel Transfer Pool** — facilitates the fuel transfer operation between the Fuel Handling Building and containment; it is also where the spent fuel transfer and shipping casks are placed for loading (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.4).

### Major Equipment Required for Refueling

- **Reactor Vessel Stud Tensioner** — a hydraulically operated device (oil working fluid) for preloading and unloading the reactor vessel closure studs at cold shutdown. <span class="hi">Three tensioners</span> are applied simultaneously to three studs 120° apart, driven by one hydraulic pumping unit; studs are tensioned to operational load in two steps to prevent high flange stresses. Relief valves on each tensioner prevent over-tensioning; micrometers measure stud elongation after tensioning (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5).
- **Reactor Vessel Head Lifting Rig (Integrated Head Assembly / IHA)** — the IHA and lifting devices are shown on Figure 9.1-6A (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5). The tripod (three vertical legs, platform assembly, sling assembly) is permanently attached to the three reactor vessel head lifting lugs. Total estimated IHA weight is approximately <span class="hi">191 tons</span> including studs/nuts/washers, or <span class="hi">172.5 tons</span> without them; the maximum drop height of the reactor vessel head is <span class="val-trip">39 feet</span> (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5). IHA lifting rig load data (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5):

  | Design load rating | <span class="hi">200 tons</span> | UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5 |
  | Maximum operating load | <span class="hi">172.5 tons</span> | UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5 |

- **Missile Shield Structure Lifting Rig** — the IHA missile shield (including the IHA lifting device) is shown in Figure 9.1-7A. The missile shield is a <span class="hi">181-inch diameter, 2-inch thick steel plate</span> that is part of the IHA (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5).
- **Reactor Internals Lifting Rig** — a three-legged structural frame connecting the main crane hook to the upper or lower internals package, connected to the internals flanges by screw threads (Figure 9.1-9). Maximum drop height of the core barrel assembly is <span class="hi">69 feet</span> (limit of polar crane hook travel with the upper core barrel and lifting rig attached). Maximum operating load is approximately <span class="hi">171 tons</span>; maximum polar crane capacity is <span class="hi">230 tons</span> (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5).
- **Manipulator Crane** — a rectilinear bridge-and-trolley crane with a vertical mast extending into the refueling water; the bridge spans the reactor cavity on rails set into the cavity-edge floor. A long tube with a <span class="hi-exam">pneumatic gripper</span> is lowered from the mast to grip the fuel assembly; a trolley-mounted winch raises the gripper tube and fuel up into the mast tube for transport. All controls are on a trolley console; bridge/trolley/winch drives are variable speed with a separate inching control on the winch. In an emergency, the bridge, trolley, and winch can be operated manually using a handwheel on the motor shaft (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5). Manipulator crane safety features/interlocks are detailed under **Operation** below.
- **Fuel Handling Crane** — a semi-gantry crane used to transport new or spent fuel assemblies between shipping container, storage racks, and upending device, via a special tool suspended from the crane hook. When handling spent fuel, the hook travel and tool length are designed to limit the maximum lift of a fuel assembly to a safe shielding depth. It is a separate structure from the spent fuel pool bridge (can be coupled to travel as a single unit) and is designed as <span class="hi">Seismic Category 2</span> to remain intact during a design basis seismic event (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5).
- **Fuel Transfer System** — an electrically driven underwater conveyor car (Figure 9.1-5) running on tracks from the refueling canal through the transfer tube into the transfer pool. The car receives a fuel assembly vertically from the manipulator crane, lowers it to horizontal for passage through the tube, then raises it to vertical in the transfer pool. During plant operation the conveyor car is stored in the refueling canal, a blind flange is bolted on the transfer tube to seal containment, and a valve seals the transfer tube on the Fuel Building side (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5).
- **New Fuel Elevator** — lowers new fuel into the transfer pool; the carriage (basket) is also designed to support/position fuel during fuel repairs and to handle new assemblies during transfer. When used for repairs, elevator hard stops are installed on the rails above the basket to prevent inadvertently raising an irradiated assembly above a safe shielding water depth (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5).
- **Cask Handling Crane (CHC)** — a bridge-and-trolley crane used to: transfer new fuel containers from the truck bay to the lay-down area, move spent fuel shipping casks from the transfer pool to the decontamination pit and truck bay, and move spent fuel transfer casks from the transfer pool to the decontamination pit and truck bay. It complies with ASME NOG-1-2004, and is single-failure-proof (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5). The main hoist wire ropes are rated for <span class="hi">115 tons</span> and the auxiliary hoist for <span class="hi">10 tons</span> (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5). CHC load data (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5):

- **Polar Gantry Crane (Containment Crane)** — fabricated by Whiting Corporation; in detailed description it is the same as the cask handling crane except for 16 parts of cable supporting the block, two independent electric braking systems with eddy-current brakes for control braking, and Class 162 resistors (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5). - **Rod Cluster Control Changing Fixture** — transfers RCC elements between fuel assemblies; comprises five major subassemblies: frame and track structure, carriage, guide tube, gripper, and drive mechanism. The carriage rides the tracks horizontally, with three compartments (two hold individual fuel assemblies, one supports a single RCC element). The pneumatically actuated gripper engages the RCC element. The fixture is located in containment and, not being near the spent fuel pool, presents no likelihood of dropping/damaging stored fuel (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5).
- **Spent Fuel Shipping Cask / Transfer Cask / Lift Yoke** — the dual-purpose MPC for dry storage is certified for storage in a HI-STORM overpack (10CFR72, CoC 1014) and transport in a HI-STAR overpack (10CFR71, CoC 9261). Dry storage uses the HI-TRAC transfer cask inside the Fuel Handling Building, lifted only with a single-failure-proof lifting system (the CHC plus a lift yoke) per NUREG-0612 §5.1.6; there are no lift height limits for the transfer cask when lifted by the CHC system. The lift yoke engages the CHC hook at top and the two HI-TRAC lifting trunnions via lift links at the bottom (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5).

## Operation

### Refueling Operation & Procedure (summary)

The refueling operation follows a detailed procedure. The summary sequence (the actual procedure sequence and details may differ) (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.3):

**Preparation** — reactor shut down, borated to refueling concentration, cooled to ambient; radiation survey and containment entry; CRDM cables disconnected; head insulation removed; canal drain valves closed and fuel transfer tube flange removed; in-core instrumentation thimble guides disconnected at the seal table and extracted downward; head nuts loosened with the hydraulic tensioner; head studs removed to storage; guide studs installed and remaining stud holes plugged; reactor vessel cavity seal installed; fuel transfer device and manipulator crane checked out; cavity filled to vessel flange; IHA unseated and raised 1 foot with the polar crane and taken to the storage pedestal; cavity filled to refueling level; control rod drive shafts unlatched; internals lifting rig lowered and latched to the support plate; internals lifted out and placed in the underwater storage rack; the core is ready for refueling (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.3).

**Refueling** — Core Shuffle: spent fuel removed from core to the Fuel Transfer System for the spent fuel pool; partially spent fuel transferred among intermediate/center/outer regions; fresh assemblies brought from the spent fuel pool through the Transfer System into the outer region. <span class="hi-exam">Whenever fuel is added to the core, source range counts are monitored to ensure unexpected count rate increases do not occur</span> (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.3). A Full Core Offload alternative systematically unloads all fuel to the Transfer System, performs insert changeouts in the spent fuel pool with the RCCA change fixture, BPRA handling tool and thimble plug handling tool, then reloads (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.3).

**Reactor Reassembly** — fuel transfer car parked and fuel transfer tube isolation valve closed; internals package replaced in vessel; full-length CRD shafts relatched to RCC elements; manipulator crane parked; O-ring grooves cleaned and new O-rings installed; cavity drained; flange cleaned; IHA lowered and seated; vessel-to-cavity seal vented and removed; head lifted for final inspection; guide studs removed; head studs installed and retorqued; canal drain valves opened and fuel transfer tube flange installed; in-core flux thimbles re-inserted; CRDM leads reconnected; head insulation installed; control rod drives checked; equipment access door closed and sealed (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.3).

### Manipulator Crane Interlocks & Safety Features

Beyond the bridge/trolley travel limit switches, the manipulator crane incorporates these safety features (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5):
1. Bridge, trolley, and winch drives are mutually interlocked to prevent simultaneous operation of any two drives (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5).
2. Bridge and trolley main motor drive operation is prevented except when the <span class="hi-exam">GRIPPER TUBE UP position switch</span> is actuated (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5).
3. The engage/disengage solenoid valves in the gripper air line are de-energized except when zero suspended weight is indicated by a force gage; a mechanical weight-actuated lock in the gripper provides backup, preventing gripper operation under load even if air pressure is applied (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5).
4. The hoist drive circuit in the up direction is opened when the GRIPPER IS DISENGAGED or when the EXCESSIVE SUSPENDED WEIGHT switch is actuated by a loading in excess of <span class="hi-exam">110 percent of a fuel assembly weight</span> (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5).
5. The hoist drive circuit (either direction) operates only when either the OPEN or CLOSED indicating switch or the gripper is actuated (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5).
6. Bridge and trolley drives are interlocked in the direction of the Transfer System so the bridge cannot travel beyond the core area unless the trolley is aligned with the refueling canal centerline; the trolley drive is locked out when the bridge is moved beyond the edge of the core (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5).

The manipulator crane is designed to prevent disengagement of a fuel assembly from the gripper under the Design Basis Earthquake (DBE); suitable restraints prevent the bridge and trolley structures from derailing under the DBE (UFSAR §9.1.4.1.5).

### Design Evaluation — Reactivity & Shielding

During reactor vessel head removal and while loading/unloading fuel, boron concentration is maintained at the most restrictive of (UFSAR §9.1.4.2):
- a k-effective of <span class="val-trip">0.95 or less</span> at All Rods In (ARI), Cold Zero Power (CZP) with a 1% Δk/k uncertainty added;
- a k-effective of <span class="val-trip">0.99 or less</span> at All Rods Out (ARO), CZP with a 1% Δk/k uncertainty added;
- a boron concentration of <span class="hi-exam">≥2000 ppm</span>, which includes a 50 ppm conservative allowance for uncertainties (UFSAR §9.1.4.2).

Adequate shielding is provided by conducting all spent fuel transfer and storage operations under water, maintaining radiation levels less than <span class="val-normal">2.5 mR/hr</span> for periodic occupancy by operating personnel; pool water level is monitored and must be pumped out since there are no gravity drains (UFSAR §9.1.4.2). Gamma radiation levels in containment and fuel storage areas are continuously monitored, providing an audible alarm at the initiating detector for an unsafe condition (UFSAR §9.1.4.2).

<span class="hi-exam">Direct communication between the Control Room and the refueling cavity manipulator crane is available whenever changes in core geometry are taking place</span> (UFSAR §9.1.4.2). When core geometry is being changed, core subcritical neutron flux is continuously monitored by at least two neutron monitors, each with continuous visual indication and one with audible indication in containment; when core geometry is not being changed, at least one neutron flux monitor is in service (UFSAR §9.1.4.2). At least one residual heat removal pump is operable (used to maintain a uniform boron concentration), and one charging pump capable of injecting borated water to the reactor coolant is available at all times (UFSAR §9.1.4.2).

The RWST capacity is <span class="hi-exam">400000 gallons</span>; for the initial fuel loading a shutdown k-effective of 0.90 or less is required, obtained with refueling water at a boron concentration of 2000 ppm (UFSAR §9.1.4.2).

### Analysis of Load-Drop Accidents

Maximum drop heights are set by the limit of polar crane hook travel; no components are lifted higher than necessary for maneuverability. The total analyzed IHA drop weight is <span class="hi">369.9 Kips</span> and the drop height considered is <span class="val-trip">39 ft</span> (the actual height to clear the lift rig for the internals); the bounding case is the inclined drop with impact above the supported hot leg nozzle, giving a calculated ductility demand of 18.7 — acceptable as it is below the maximum allowable value of 20.0 (UFSAR §9.1.4.3). It is very unlikely a dropped vessel head would disrupt coolant flow; loss of refueling water above the seal has no safety significance since no fuel handling is in progress during head handling. During a postulated drop of the head or upper core barrel onto the vessel, some fuel rods may fail, but the radiation monitoring system will detect the released gas and immediately isolate the reactor containment (UFSAR §9.1.4.3). No heavy loads are handled over equipment required for safe shutdown during fuel movement or cask movement (UFSAR §9.1.4.3).

### Tests, Inspections & Dry Spent Fuel Storage

Prior to initial fueling, preoperational checkouts of the fuel handling equipment were performed; prior to subsequent refuelings the equipment is inspected for operability and certain components (fuel transfer car, manipulator crane) are operated to ensure reliable performance before moving irradiated fuel (UFSAR §9.1.4.4). After sufficient storage time in the spent fuel pool, spent fuel may be moved to dry storage at the on-site ISFSI (located north of Hope Creek), operated under the general license of 10CFR72 Subpart K using the NRC-certified HI-STORM 100 System (CoC 1014, listed in 10CFR72.214) (UFSAR §9.1.4.5).
