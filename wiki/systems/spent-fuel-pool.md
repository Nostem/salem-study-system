# Spent Fuel Pool

## Function & Design Basis

The Spent Fuel Pool (SFP) Cooling System is designed to remove the decay heat generated by spent fuel elements stored in the spent fuel pool, which is located in the Fuel Handling Building adjacent to the Containment Building. A secondary function is to clarify and purify the spent fuel pool, transfer pool, and refueling water. The system design considers the need to totally unload a reactor at the time when spent fuel is in the fuel pool. (UFSAR §9.1.3.1) The description applies to Unit 1, with Unit 2 having an identical system. (UFSAR §9.1.3.1)

The system design incorporates redundant active components and is designed with anti-siphon holes to prevent draining of the spent fuel pool below the top of the stored fuel elements. (UFSAR §9.1.3.1) The system maintains the spent fuel pool at normal temperatures, and boron concentration in the pool fluid is maintained at a minimum of <span class="hi">800 ppm</span>. (UFSAR §9.1.3.1)

Borated water is used to fill the spent fuel storage pool at a concentration to match that used in the reactor cavity and refueling canal during refueling operations, with sufficient center-to-center spacing so that Keff ≤ 0.95 even if unborated water is used to fill the pool (based on 17 x 17 fuel). (UFSAR §9.1.2.3) One mislocated fuel bundle has been found acceptable as long as the soluble boron concentration is maintained above <span class="hi">600 ppm</span> in the Spent Fuel Pool. (UFSAR §9.1.2.3) The maximum Spent Fuel Pool neutron multiplication with the maximum density racks installed will not exceed Keff ≤ 0.95 with unborated water. (UFSAR §9.1.2.3)

With the 1994 rerack, spent fuel storage capacity was increased from 1170 fuel assemblies to 1632 fuel assemblies. (UFSAR §9.1.3.2)

## Key Components

The Spent Fuel Pool Cooling System consists of three subsystems: the Cooling System, the Purification System, and the Skimmer System. The cooling loop consists of the spent fuel pool pumps and the spent fuel pool heat exchanger. The purification loop consists of the spent fuel pool pump, the spent fuel pool filter, the spent fuel pool demineralizer, the refueling water purification pump, and the refueling water purification filter. The skimmer loop consists of the skimmer pump, strainer, and filter. (UFSAR §9.1.3.2) All piping in contact with spent fuel pool water is austenitic stainless steel. (UFSAR §9.1.3.2)

## Automatic Features & Setpoints

There are no automatic SFP cooling pump trips and no automatic makeup to the SFP; the spent fuel pool pumps are operated manually from a local station. (UFSAR §9.1.3.2)

Pool water level indication is provided by individual high and low water level alarms. The alarms are actuated by deviation from normal water level (Elevation <span class="hi">128 feet 8 inches</span>) of plus or minus <span class="val-alarm">6 inches</span>. The alarms are annunciated in the Fuel Handling Building at the spent fuel pool and in the Control Room. (UFSAR §9.1.3.3) Annunciation of an alarm is confirmed by visually checking the spent fuel pool water level; alarms may be expected to occur occasionally due to gradual changes in pool water temperature and surface evaporation. (UFSAR §9.1.3.3) A high sump level alarm for the Fuel Handling Building sump is provided in the Control Room. (UFSAR §9.1.3.3)

Gamma radiation is continuously monitored in the Fuel Handling Building; a high-level signal is alarmed locally and is annunciated in the Control Rooms. (UFSAR §9.1.2.3)

## Design Features & Interlocks

During the heat removal operation, fuel pool water flows from the spent fuel pool to a spent fuel pool pump suction, is pumped through the tube side of the heat exchanger, and is returned to the pool. The suction line, protected by a strainer, is located at an elevation <span class="hi">4 feet below the pool normal water level</span>, while the return line terminates in the pool at an elevation <span class="hi">approximately 6 feet above the top of the fuel assemblies</span>. If the spent fuel pool pump fails, the second pump supplies 100-percent backup. (UFSAR §9.1.3.2)

The most serious failure of this system would be complete loss of water in the spent fuel pool. To protect against this, the spent fuel pool cooling suction connection enters near the normal water level so that the pool cannot be gravity-drained, and the cooling water return lines contain <span class="hi">anti-siphon holes</span> to prevent the possibility of gravity draining the pool. (UFSAR §9.1.3.3) There are no drains or permanently connected systems to the spent fuel pool (Seismic Class I) which, in the event of failure, could cause loss of coolant from the pool that would uncover the fuel. (UFSAR §9.1.3.3) For beyond-design-basis accident mitigation, the tell-tale liner leakoff drain lines have isolation capability to maintain spent fuel pool inventory if liner failure is anticipated. (UFSAR §9.1.3.3)

Water loss due to the accidental opening of a sluice gate when the transfer pool is empty will not occur due to redundancy in the sluice gates — two sluice gates separate the spent fuel pool from the transfer pool. (UFSAR §9.1.3.3) A heavy load handling accident would not result in water leakage severe enough to uncover the spent fuel; the maximum load carried over the spent fuel pool is that of a fuel assembly, and it is not possible to drop a fuel assembly on the spent fuel pool liner plate. (UFSAR §9.1.3.3)

Salem fuel pool cooling systems were designed with substantial reliability, with original design features including seismic Class II piping stress analysis (upgraded to Class I in 1981), location in seismic Category I buildings, safety-related heat exchangers, a safety-related heat sink (CCW to service water), redundant pumps, and electric power from independent 460 V 1E buses backed by EDGs. (UFSAR §9.1.3.3) Following a 1995 self-assessment, a seismic upgrade of the Spent Fuel Pool Cooling System rendered pool temperatures above <span class="hi">180°F</span> non-credible; the evaluation concluded pool temperature would be maintained 180°F and below under normal, abnormal, and accident conditions. (UFSAR §9.1.3.3)

Use of the heat-exchanger cross connect is controlled by procedures: four manual valves (two per unit) must be opened to cross connect the Unit 1 and Unit 2 heat exchangers. Before placing a high heat load in a pool that would require parallel heat exchangers, or before taking one heat exchanger out of service, the heat loads in the spent fuel pool(s) are evaluated to ensure they can tolerate a temporary interruption of cooling. (UFSAR §9.1.3.3)

While the heat removal operation is in progress, a portion of the spent fuel pool water (<span class="hi">100 gpm</span>) may be diverted through the spent fuel pool demineralizer and spent fuel pool filter to maintain spent fuel pool water clarity and purity. (UFSAR §9.1.3.2) The demineralizer is sized to pass 100 gpm of the loop circulation flow to provide adequate purification of the fuel pool water for unrestricted access to the working area and to maintain optical clarity. (UFSAR §9.1.3.2)

## Interconnections & Loads

The spent fuel pool heat exchanger is cooled by Component Cooling Water, which circulates through the shell side while spent fuel pool water circulates through the tubes. (UFSAR §9.1.3.2, Table 9.1-2) The safety-related heat sink for the system is CCW to service water. (UFSAR §9.1.3.3)

Boron may be added to the pool from the Chemical and Volume Control System (CVCS). Borated water may be supplied from the RWST via the refueling water purification pump connection, or by placing a temporary line from the boric acid blender (located in the CVCS) directly into the pool. (UFSAR §9.1.3.2)

The refueling water purification pump circulates water in an isolatable loop between the refueling water storage tank (RWST) and the spent fuel pool demineralizer and filter; borated refueling water may be pumped from either the RWST or the refueling cavity, through the demineralizer and filter, and discharged to either the refueling cavity or the RWST. (UFSAR §9.1.3.2) The pool water may be separated from the transfer pool by a sluice gate; excess water from the spent fuel pool is directed to a holdup tank in the CVCS or to the decontamination pit for temporary storage. (UFSAR §9.1.3.2)

A Controlled Ventilation System (Fuel Handling Area Ventilation) removes gaseous radioactivity from the atmosphere in the Fuel Handling and Auxiliary Buildings and discharges it to the atmosphere via the plant vent; radiation monitors actuate high-activity alarms in the Control Rooms. (UFSAR §9.1.2.3)

## Effects of Loss / Malfunction

A failure analysis of system pumps, heat exchangers, and valves is presented in UFSAR Table 9.1-3. If a spent fuel pool pump stops running and cannot be restarted, the remaining full-capacity pump can be brought into operation. (UFSAR Table 9.1-3) Rupture of a pump casing is not considered credible — the casing and shell are designed for 150 psi and 200°F (exceeding maximum operating conditions), the pump is inspectable, and it is located in the auxiliary building protected against credible accidents. (UFSAR Table 9.1-3) Heat exchanger tube or shell rupture is considered incredible because of the low operating pressure. (UFSAR Table 9.1-3) If the skimmer pump stops and cannot be restarted, spent fuel assemblies continue to be cooled by the spent fuel pool pump; pool water may become slightly murky until the pump is restored, and clarity is maintained to some extent by bypassing pool water through the demineralizer. (UFSAR Table 9.1-3)

In the event of a sustained loss of forced cooling (e.g. a design basis earthquake under the original non-seismically-qualified design), decay heat would have been removed by pool boiling. (UFSAR §9.1.3.3) If a leaking fuel assembly is stored in the pool, a small quantity of fission products may enter the cooling water; these and other contaminants are removed by the spent fuel pool purification loop. (UFSAR §9.1.3.3)

Refueling decay-heat analyses (UFSAR §9.1.3.2) bound the peak pool temperature: with one pump and one heat exchanger in operation, a normal partial-batch discharge (Case 1, 88 assemblies) peaks at <span class="hi">149°F</span> about 195 hours after shutdown, while a full-core offload (Cases 2 and 3, 193 assemblies) peaks at <span class="hi">180°F</span> about 204–205 hours after shutdown. (UFSAR §9.1.3.2) The Spent Fuel Pool Integrated Decay Heat Management Program performs a pre-outage assessment of pool heat loads and heatup rates; prior to each refueling it demonstrates that water temperature will not exceed <span class="hi">149°F</span> following full core offload using only one heat exchanger per pool, and validates availability of both spent fuel pool heat exchangers (each with an available pit pump) for a full core offload. (UFSAR §9.1.3.2)

## Control-Room Operation

The normal source of makeup water to the spent fuel pool is the Demineralized Water System, which distributes water from two 500000-gallon demineralized water tanks (the tanks and distribution system are not seismically classified). (UFSAR §9.1.3.3) Makeup is also available from the primary water storage tank via the primary water makeup pumps (Seismic Class II) and from the CVCS holdup tanks via the holdup tank recirculation pump (Seismic Class II). (UFSAR §9.1.3.3) For beyond-design-basis scenarios, a portable pump with appropriate connections and hose can deliver approximately <span class="hi">100 gpm</span> makeup from one of the RWSTs (each <span class="hi">364500 gallons</span>, Class I seismic) directly to the spent fuel pool; the installed RWST valves are kept locked, closed and capped under administrative control. (UFSAR §9.1.3.3) Up to <span class="hi">100 gpm</span> of makeup is also available from the RWST via the refueling water purification loop. (UFSAR §9.1.3.3)

The spent fuel pool pumps and refueling water purification pump are operated manually from a local station; manual stop valves isolate equipment and lines and manual throttle valves provide flow control. (UFSAR §9.1.3.2) Active components are in continuous use during normal plant operation and require no additional periodic tests; periodic visual inspections and preventive maintenance are conducted following normal industrial practice. (UFSAR §9.1.3.4)

Action: suspend all fuel assembly movement and crane operations with loads in the fuel storage areas, restore level within 4 hours. (TS 3/4.9.11, Amend 282)
- **TS 3/4.9.12 — Fuel Handling Ventilation System**: FHV OPERABLE with two exhaust fans and one supply fan operable and operating, AND capable of maintaining slightly negative pressure in the Fuel Handling Building; applicable during movement of irradiated fuel within the storage pool. (TS 3/4.9.12)
- **TS 3/4.9.1 — Boron Concentration**: boron concentration of the RCS, fuel storage pool, refueling canal, and refueling cavity maintained within the COLR limit in Mode 6 (the fuel storage pool, refueling canal, and refueling cavity only when connected to the RCS). (TS 3/4.9.1, Amend 282)
