# 13KV

## Function & Design Basis

The 13 kV system is the intermediate preferred (offsite) power distribution level at Salem. The four 500-13 kV station power transformers (T1, T2, T3, T4) connect to different bus sections of the 500 kV switching station, and each has the capacity to start both units at the same time (UFSAR §8.3.1.1). From the 13 kV buses, 13-4 kV station power transformers step the voltage down to feed the 4160 V group, vital, and circulating water bus sections (UFSAR §8.3.1.1).

The 4160 V vital buses, which feed safeguards equipment, are energized by station power transformers served by the 13 kV south bus sections; preferred power is supplied to the 13 kV bus south sections by two sources from the switchyard (UFSAR §8.1.2). The 13 kV bus arrangements assure a continuous preferred power supply to each unit in the event one 500-13 kV transformer should become inoperable (UFSAR §8.3.1.1).

## Key Components

- **Station power transformers T1, T2, T3, T4** — 500-13.8 kV, rated 60/80/100 MVA, 112 MVA at 65°C (UFSAR Figure 8.2-2). Connected to different bus sections of the 500 kV switching station; each can start both units simultaneously (UFSAR §8.3.1.1).
- **13 kV north ring bus** — sectionalizing breakers are normally open; 500-13 kV transformer T1 and T2 each feeds two (one for each unit) 13-4 kV station power transformers T11, T21 and T12, T22 associated with the group buses (UFSAR §8.3.1.1, Figure 8.3-2).
- **13 kV south bus** — each isolated section (A and D) is powered from 500-13 kV transformer T3 and T4 respectively and feeds two (one for each unit) 13-4 kV station power transformers T13, T24 and T14, T23 associated with vital buses and circulating water switchgear (UFSAR §8.3.1.1, Figure 8.3-2A).
- **13-4 kV station power transformers** — step 13 kV down to 4160 V. T13 and T14 (Unit 1) share the loads of three vital buses and two CW bus sections; T23 and T24 (Unit 2) share the loads of three vital buses and two CW bus sections (UFSAR §8.3.1.1).
- **25-4-4 kV auxiliary power transformer** — primary side connected to the generator isolated phase bus; each of its two 4 kV secondary windings connects to two 4160 V group buses (UFSAR §8.3.1.1). (Not part of the 13 kV path; feeds the group buses directly from the main generator once synchronized.)

## Operation

The 500-13 kV station power transformers supply the station electrical requirements as the preferred (offsite) source; with the 13 kV bus sections sectionalized as described, the loss of any one 500-13 kV transformer still leaves a continuous preferred supply to each unit (UFSAR §8.3.1.1). The 4160 V group buses are energized by the 13-4 kV station power transformers during startup; after the generator is synchronized to the 500 kV system, the group buses are manually transferred to the 25-4-4 kV auxiliary power transformer, and should the unit trip each 4160 V group bus automatically transfers from the auxiliary power transformer source back to the station power transformer source (UFSAR §8.3.1.2).

The vital buses are fed directly from the 13-4 kV station power transformers T13, T14 (Unit 1) and T23, T24 (Unit 2); during normal operation two of the vital buses are supplied from one station power transformer and the third from the other station power transformer (UFSAR §8.3.1.2).

An Open Phase Protection System (OPPS) is installed at Salem 1 and 2 to monitor for open phase conditions at the 500-13.8 kV station power transformers and at the 13 kV to 4 kV station service transformers, in accordance with NRC Bulletin 2012-01, "Design Vulnerability in Electric Power Systems" (ADAMS ML12074A115). The OPPS detects and alerts operators of an open phase condition on either the 500 kV system or the 13.8 kV system, allowing them to manually transfer the Class 1E 4 kV bus from the faulted offsite source to the non-faulted offsite source; the automatic bus transfer (trip) function of the OPPS is permanently defeated by opening the test switches in the OPPS trip logic circuitry, leaving it in alarm-only mode (UFSAR §8.3.1.2).
