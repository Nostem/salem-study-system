# 115V AC

## Function & Design Basis

Four 115-V ac vital instrument buses (1/2 A, B, C & D) supply regulated, uninterruptible AC power to reactor control and protection instrumentation and safety-related equipment. Each bus is fed from an individual Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS) so that the four buses form redundant channels for reactor control and protection instrumentation (UFSAR §8.3.1.4). The four buses correspond to the four protection channels (A, B, C, D); the 1D / 2D bus and its power-supply channel share the B source, while the 1A/2A, 1B/2B, and 1C/2C buses carry channels A, B, and C respectively (UFSAR Table 8.3-1).

The 115-V ac instrument buses are supplied through inverters from the unit's 125-V dc vital station battery system, which provides power to safeguards loads and, through these inverters, to the 115-V ac instrument buses (UFSAR §8.1.2). The 115-V ac Control Power System for Units 1 and 2 is illustrated on Plant Drawing 211370 (UFSAR §8.3.1.4).

## Key Components

- **Uninterruptible Power Supplies (UPS), one per bus** — each UPS contains a Rectifier, an Inverter, an ac Line Regulator, and a Static Switch. The Rectifier receives the normal source (vital 230-V ac power), converts (rectifies ac/dc) it to dc power, and then the Inverter converts (inverts dc/ac) it to ac power to supply the bus (UFSAR §8.3.1.4).
- **ac Line Regulator** — receives the alternate source, vital 230-V ac power from the same normal-source vital 230-V ac bus, and supplies a regulated 115-V ac output that backs up the Inverter (UFSAR §8.3.1.4).
- **Static Switch** — transfers the vital instrument bus loads between the Inverter output and the ac Line Regulator output (UFSAR §8.3.1.4).
- **125-V dc vital station battery** — automatically supplies the UPS Inverter on loss of the 230-V ac normal source or a Rectifier malfunction, maintaining uninterruptible ac output power (UFSAR §8.3.1.4). The per-bus channel assignments for the Battery feed, the 230V CC normal feed, and the 230V CC emergency feed are given in UFSAR Table 8.3-1.

## Operation

Under normal operation each UPS Rectifier draws the normal source (vital 230-V ac), rectifies it to dc, and the Inverter reconverts that dc to ac to power its vital instrument bus (UFSAR §8.3.1.4). In the event of a 230-V ac power loss or a Rectifier malfunction, the <span class="hi-exam">125-V dc vital station battery power automatically supplies the UPS Inverter</span>, maintaining uninterruptible ac output power (UFSAR §8.3.1.4).

In the event of a UPS Inverter malfunction, the Static Switch senses a loss of Inverter output voltage and <span class="hi-exam">automatically fast-transfers the associated vital instrument bus loads to the ac Line Regulator 115-V ac output</span> (the alternate source). When the Inverter voltage returns to normal, the Static Switch automatically returns the associated vital instrument bus loads to the Inverter output (UFSAR §8.3.1.4).

The Safeguards Equipment Control (SEC) System, which controls loading of the diesel generators, takes its control power for each train (A, B, and C) from the 115-V ac instrument bus in that train (UFSAR §8.3.1.5.2).

### Bus Transfers & UPS Operation

<div class="callout callout-exam">
<div class="callout-label">Exam — 2012 Q57</div>
A <span class="hi-exam">latched transfer of a VIB inverter takes 2/3 of one cycle = 11.1 milli-seconds</span>, which is too fast for control room instrumentation to respond — instrumentation powered from the bus is <span class="hi-exam">unaffected by the transfer</span>. The VIB and its instrumentation <span class="hi-exam">remain OPERABLE as long as the inverter is powering the Vital Bus</span> (no INOPERABLE declaration needed; indication is not lost). <span class="hi-trap">1D VIB is powered from the 1B bus — the C and D VIB inverters are NOT fed from a common 230 VAC source, so a 1C transfer does not momentarily lose 1D.</span>
</div>

<div class="callout callout-exam">
<div class="callout-label">Exam — 2015 Q14</div>
2B VIB UPS source transfers: the static switch is moved from Normal to Alternate by placing the <span class="hi-exam">Test Transfer switch to ALT (SO.115-0012 Section 5.4)</span>; the Manual Bypass Switch in <span class="hi-exam">Bypass to Alternate</span> positions contacts <span class="hi-exam">B1, B2, B4 closed and B3, B5 open</span>. With the static switch in Bypass to Alternate, the VIB is fed from the <span class="hi-exam">AC Line Regulator (Alternate source)</span> — so if the 2B 230VAC-to-AC-Line-Regulator breaker opens, the <span class="hi-exam">2B VIB DEENERGIZES</span>. <span class="hi-trap">Automatic transfer is NOT available (static switch not in Normal), and the inverter output cannot reach the bus with B3/B5 open. Restoration is MANUAL: place the Static Switch in Normal and the Test Transfer switch to N (Sections 5.7.3/5.7.4) — NOT Isolate (Alternate).</span>
</div>

### System Impacts on Loss of VIB

<div class="callout callout-exam">
<div class="callout-label">Exam — 2015 Q42</div>
The 2B Vital Instrument Bus powers SSPS Train B; if it is deenergized, <span class="hi-exam">SSPS Train B slave relays would not actuate on a Safety Injection signal</span>. (SSPS Train A 45VDC power is supplied from A and D vital power, so Train A is unaffected.) See [[RPS/SSPS]].
</div>

<div class="callout callout-exam">
<div class="callout-label">Exam — 2016 Q20</div>
Loss of 2A 115VAC Vital Instrument Bus impact on AFW: <span class="hi-exam">23AF21 and 24AF21 fail closed (pressure override) because power to the AFW pump discharge pressure transmitter is lost</span>, and <span class="hi-exam">power is also lost to the AF21 valve controllers</span>. AB.115-0001 Step 13 dispatches an NEO to locally throttle 23AF21 and 24AF21. <span class="hi-trap">PRESSURE OVERRIDE DEFEAT cannot recover AF21 control because the loss of VIB also de-energizes the valve controllers themselves.</span>
</div>

<div class="callout callout-exam">
<div class="callout-label">Exam — 2016 Q39</div>
Vital Instrument Bus naming and SEC mapping: each train SEC (A, B, C) is powered from its respective Vital Instrument Bus — <span class="hi-exam">2AVIB24, 2BVIB27, 2CVIB9</span>. With SEC BLOCK switches inoperable, deenergizing a VIB removes the SEC inhibit so that a piece of equipment held off by an unsatisfied SEC permissive can be started locally. To start 21 Charging Pump (B), 21 SI Pump (A), and #2 ECAC (C), all three VIBs must be deenergized.
</div>

<div class="callout callout-exam">
<div class="callout-label">Exam — 2019 Q34</div>
Steam Dump I/P converters are powered by <span class="hi-exam">C/D 115 VAC Instrument Bus</span>. Loss of 115 VAC to the I/P converters causes steam dumps to fail closed.
</div>

<div class="callout callout-exam">
<div class="callout-label">Exam — 2020 Q12</div>
Loss of 2A 115V Vital Instrument Bus affects VCT level instrument LT-112 (loss of indication, alarms, and auto makeup). LT-114 remains available but <span class="hi-exam">only via the plant computer</span> in the control room (NOT on the control console) and at Panel 216 in charging pump alley.
</div>

<div class="callout callout-exam">
<div class="callout-label">Exam — 2020 Q88</div>
Loss of 2B VIB impact on ESFAS: Containment Spray Hi-Hi Containment Pressure bistables are <span class="hi-exam">energized to actuate</span>. Loss of the 2B VIB causes the associated bistable to remain de-energized → <span class="hi-exam">logic goes from 2/4 to 2/3</span>. Per TS 3.3.2.1, the inoperable channel is <span class="hi-exam">BYPASSED (not tripped)</span> to prevent spurious CS actuation. <span class="hi-trap">Most ESF bistables are de-energized to trip → tripped on inoperability. CS bistables are the exception — energized to actuate → bypassed on inoperability.</span>
</div>
