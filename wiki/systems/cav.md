# CAV

## Function & Design Basis

The Control Area Air Conditioning System (CAACS) and the Control Room Emergency Air Conditioning System (CREACS) maintain room temperatures within the limits required for operation, maintenance, and testing of plant controls, and permit continuous occupancy under normal and design accident conditions (UFSAR §9.4.1). The CREACS is a portion of the CAACS that operates to ensure continuous occupancy of operating personnel in the Control Room Envelope (CRE) under emergency conditions (UFSAR §9.4.1.2).

The CAACS maintains control room area ambient temperatures of <span class="val-normal">76°F dry bulb</span> and a maximum of <span class="val-normal">50 percent relative humidity</span>, based on outside air temperatures ranging from 0°F winter dry bulb to 95°F summer dry bulb and 78°F summer wet bulb during normal and emergency conditions (UFSAR §9.4.1.1). The CREACS maintains room ambient temperatures inside the CRE within <span class="val-normal">55° to 85°F</span> during emergency conditions, except for the Data Logging Rooms which are maintained within <span class="val-normal">55° to 95°F</span> (UFSAR §9.4.1.1).

The CAACS/CREACS design bases also provide for (UFSAR §9.4.1.1):

- Protection of the CRE and control room areas (relay and control equipment rooms) from infiltration of fire, smoke, or airborne radioactivity by minimum leakage penetrations, weather stripped doors, absence of outside windows, and maintenance of a <span class="hi">positive pressure during normal operation</span>.
- Protection of the CRE from a radiological design basis accident by filtering airborne activity and maintaining the CRE at a combination of <span class="val-normal">1/8 inwc and 1/16 inwc</span> positive pressure differential above the outside environment and adjacent rooms. The dp is <span class="hi-exam">1/8 inwc for all adjacent areas except the relay rooms, which is 1/16 inwc</span> (UFSAR §9.4.1.1).
- Protection from airborne toxic gas or hazardous chemical releases outside the control room area.
- Protection from smoke generated inside or outside the control room area.
- Maintaining ambient temperatures within <span class="val-normal">55° to 85°F</span> in the control room and adjoining equipment room; <span class="val-normal">65° to 85°F</span> in the Relay Room and Unit 2 125 VDC Battery Rooms; and <span class="val-normal">65° to 95°F</span> in the Unit 1 125 VDC Battery Rooms during normal plant operating conditions (UFSAR §9.4.1.1).
- Remaining operable during a design basis seismic event (UFSAR §9.4.1.1).

The CRE for each unit consists of the unit control room, a data logging room, a control equipment room, and a relay room. The operator ready room, conference room, control room supervisor's office areas, and both units' control rooms are located in a common protected area, the CRE, which is serviced by both units' CAACS during normal conditions and both units' CREACS during emergency conditions (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). The control area is enclosed in a Class I (seismic) structure (UFSAR §9.4.1.2).

## Key Components

**CAACS (per unit)** — a filter enclosure equipped with medium and low efficiency filters, three vane-axial fans (one standby), a multi-zone coil unit with three cooling coils and one heating coil, air distribution ducts, and electrically or pneumatically operated dampers (with manual backup operators). Each CAACS primarily serves its unit's equipment room and relay room areas; the operator ready room, control room supervisor areas, and both units' data logging and control rooms are serviced by both units' CAACS during normal operation (UFSAR §9.4.1.2).

**CREACS (per unit)** — a portion of the CAACS, consisting of a filter enclosure equipped with HEPA and charcoal filters, a cooling coil, two vane-axial fans (one standby), supply and return ducts to the CRE, outside air intakes (crosstied between Unit 1 and Unit 2 CREACS filter units), a distribution plenum above the control room conference room, and electrically or pneumatically and manually operated dampers (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). The CREACS supplies cooled HEPA and charcoal filtered air to the CRE when actuated by the Solid State Protection System (SSPS) or control room intake high radiation signal (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). The HEPA filters have a removal efficiency of <span class="val-normal">95%</span> and the charcoal filters have a removal efficiency for radioiodine of <span class="val-normal">95%</span> (UFSAR §9.4.1.3.5).

**Cooling source** — CAACS and CREACS cooling coils are supplied with chilled water from the Chilled Water System located in each unit's mechanical equipment area at elevation 100 foot of the Auxiliary Building. Each unit's Chilled Water System consists of three 50% capacity package chiller units, two 100% capacity recirculating pumps, condensers cooled by the Service Water System (SWS), and interconnecting piping (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). During single CREACS train operation, the associated cooling coil is provided sufficient chilled water with two chillers in service to maintain CRE temperatures below 85°F (Data Logging Rooms below 95°F) (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). The air conditioning equipment is designed to Class I (seismic) criteria and can be energized from the standby AC power supply (UFSAR §9.4.1.2).

**Key dampers** — normal intakes CAA40, 41, 43 & 45; emergency intakes CAA48, 49, 50 & 51; exhaust CAA18 & 19; return damper CAA5; CRE boundary / CAACS–CREACS isolation dampers CAA14 and CAA20; CREACS return damper CAA17; manual makeup dampers VHE1058, VHE1130, VHE1133 and VHE1141 (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). Chilled water control valves CH74 and CH168 supply chilled water to the CAACS and CREACS coils (CH168 is permanently open) (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). Dampers required to operate during Mode 4 are designed to fail to their designated position upon loss of control air or power; CAA14, CAA17, and CAA20 are spring loaded to their fail safe positions on a loss of control air or power (UFSAR §9.4.1.2, §9.4.1.3.1).

**Radiation monitors** — two safety related outside air activity monitors per intake monitor air entering the control room supply duct; these are beta scintillation type detectors with a range of 10^1 – 10^7 cpm, with an instrument failure alarm, indication in the control room, and alarm to the control room. The same radiation monitor that generates the alarm is used for automatic initiation of Mode 4 (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). One non-safety related area monitor per unit is mounted in the control room (GM type detectors, range 10^-1 – 10^4 mR/hr Unit 1, 10^-1 – 10^6 mR/hr Unit 2) and serves to provide indication only (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). Smoke detectors are provided to detect trace amounts of combustion products (UFSAR §9.4.1.2).

## Operation

Depending on outside climatic conditions, one or two CAACS fans per unit are normally in operation, the third serving as standby. The CREACS is isolated and in standby during normal operation. The CAACS normally operates with a fixed amount of outside air to maintain a slight positive pressure in the CRE (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). The control area ventilation system has four modes of operation (UFSAR §9.4.1.2):

**Normal (Mode 1)** — the operating mode for CAACS during normal plant operations. A mixture of outside air and recirculated air is supplied to the control room areas (relay room, equipment room, and CRE). Typically one or two supply fans operate with the third as backup. Outside makeup (CAA40 & 43 open) and recirculated air is mixed and filtered through roughing filters, cooled (or heated), and supplied to the control room areas. The CRE and control room areas are maintained at a positive pressure. The CREACS is isolated and in standby (UFSAR §9.4.1.2).

**Fire Inside Control Area (Mode 2)** — manually initiated by the operators for once-through, 100% outside air operation or purge. All normal intake (CAA40, 41, 43 & 45) and exhaust dampers (CAA18 & 19) open and return damper (CAA5) closes to allow 100% outside air to be pumped through the control room areas and expelled to the outside, making the control room habitable. Up to two CAACS supply fans can operate; roughing filters filter the outside air; the CREACS is isolated and in standby (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). Purging of the control room areas is provided by the operators manually initiating the system to Mode 2 (UFSAR §9.4.1.3.6).

**Fire Outside Control Area (Mode 3)** — for airborne toxic gas, hazardous chemical releases, or smoke from outside the control room, provisions provide 100% recirculated air. All normal intakes (CAA40, 41, 43 & 45), emergency intakes (CAA48, 49, 50 & 51) and exhaust (CAA18 & 19) dampers are closed, isolating the ventilation systems from the outside environment. The Unit 1 and 2 CAACS are isolated from the CRE (by closure of CAA14 and CAA20 dampers) and operate in full recirculation supplying cool air to the relay and equipment rooms, while both units' CREACS operate to recirculate air to the CRE. Up to two CAACS supply fans and one CREACS supply fan per unit can operate; CH74 open and CH168 permanently open supply chilled water. This mode is manually initiated by the operators from both control rooms (UFSAR §9.4.1.2).

**Accident Pressurized – Two Filtration Train Alignment (Mode 4)** — provided for airborne radioactivity and long term occupancy of the control room. All normal intake (CAA40, 41, 43 & 45), exhaust (CAA18 & 19), and CRE boundary (CAA14 and CAA20) dampers are closed, isolating both units' CAACS from the outside environment and the CRE. CH168 is permanently open; CAACS operates in Mode 3 with CH74 open. <span class="hi-exam">An emergency intake from one unit will open and the opposite remain closed based on which unit initiated the accident signal.</span> Both units' CREACS filtration trains start with one fan operating in each unit; if a fan fails to start, the standby fan automatically starts (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). Each CREACS filter unit draws in <span class="val-normal">1100 scfm</span> of outside air mixing with <span class="val-normal">7000 scfm</span> recirculated air from the CRE; the total of <span class="val-normal">2200 scfm</span> makeup air ensures the CRE is pressurized to greater than a combination of 1/8 inwc and 1/16 inwc above the outside environment and adjacent rooms (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). The CAACS and CREACS automatically actuate upon an accident signal (SI or high radiation) and select the preferred emergency intake; if automatic selection of the preferred intake is unavailable, the operator can manually place the CREACS into Mode 4 with the preferred emergency intake selected to any unit at power or shutdown (UFSAR §9.4.1.2).

**System controls / automatic actuation.** Both Units 1 and 2 control room ventilation systems automatically initiate Mode 4 operation upon any one of: (1) Safety Injection signal from Unit 1, (2) Safety Injection signal from Unit 2, (3) High outside air activity from Unit 1 control room intake monitor, or (4) High outside air activity from Unit 2 control room intake monitor (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). Selection of emergency intake dampers that open during Mode 4 is based on: a Safety Injection or control room intake high radiation signal from Unit 1 will open emergency intake dampers on Unit 2 (Unit 1 remain closed), and a signal from Unit 2 will open emergency intake dampers on Unit 1 (Unit 2 remain closed) (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). <span class="hi-exam">The opposite unit's emergency intake opens because that intake is farthest from the radiological source detected by the actuating unit's monitor.</span> The dampers actuated in the CAACS and CREACS are pneumatically controlled with position indication in the control room; dampers required to operate during Mode 4 fail to their designated position upon loss of control air or power, and automatically actuate upon SSPS and radiation monitoring system (RMS) signals (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). Operators can manually initiate the CAACS and CREACS to any operating mode from the control room (UFSAR §9.4.1.2).

**Single Filtration Train (Maintenance) alignment.** The system has provisions to allow one CREACS train to be out of service for maintenance. In this standby alignment, one CREACS train on one unit is isolated and the other train is aligned (using manual dampers) for cooling the entire CRE with the CREACS train in standby. The manual dampers VHE1058, VHE1130, VHE1133 and VHE1141, when positioned, ensure a total of 2200 scfm of makeup air is provided and supply air is distributed throughout the CRE. CREACS return damper CAA17 is administratively controlled to the open position. The air intake dampers must remain capable of automatically actuating and aligning the outside air intake to the non-accident unit's intake damper upon receipt of an SI or High Radiation signal, when both units are in Modes 1-4 (UFSAR §9.4.1.2). A single CREACS filtration train is capable of providing adequate cooling, removal of airborne activity, and pressurization of the CRE during an accident within GDC 19 limits (UFSAR §9.4.1.3.8).

**Single failure / redundancy (safety evaluation).** The CAACS and CREACS provide redundant pneumatically operated isolation dampers (with manual backup operators) for isolating the outside environment from the control room areas; these fail to their designated positions for Mode 4 accident operation. Each unit's emergency intakes have dual parallel flow paths, each with redundant pneumatically operated dampers in series with a manual isolation damper for maintenance; pneumatic dampers are supplied from separate control air headers (UFSAR §9.4.1.3.1). Each CREACS filtration train has two 100% capacity supply fans supplied by the standby A/C power supply; a safety related flow switch downstream of the fan discharge duct starts the standby supply fan if the running fan fails (UFSAR §9.4.1.3.1). Each unit's controls are initiated from redundant trains of SSPS and control room intake radiation monitors, electrically interlocked with the opposite unit's controls so the opposite unit's CAACS and CREACS automatically initiate Mode 4; this interlock is electrically isolated and connects only to the same division/train on the opposite unit (UFSAR §9.4.1.3.1, §9.4.1.3.2). The shared ductwork is designed to seismic I criteria and maintains its safety function during an accident; the safety function is not impaired per GDC-5 criteria (UFSAR §9.4.1.3.2). A chiller capacity test and CWS system balance were performed, and CREACS coil inspections demonstrate the capability to remove heat loads and maintain temperatures within limits (UFSAR §9.4.1.3.7).
